What is going on?

You have been changing the seed and the breeze. Here is what those changes help you notice.

A heavy body and a papery wing

The seed’s weight is concentrated at one end. As it falls, air pushing on the uneven shape makes it turn. Try changing the wing size while keeping the weight the same.

The spinning wing slows the fall

As the wing spins through the air, it produces an upward force that slows the seed’s descent. Use slow motion to watch the rotation. A wingless seed would fall much faster.

More time for wind to carry it

A slower fall gives wind more time to move a seed away from its parent. Landing in open ground can mean more light and less competition for water and space.

Why do some seeds have wings?

Trees do not choose to grow wings on their seeds. Over many generations, inherited variations that helped seeds spread and grow could be passed on through the trees that survived and reproduced. Wings are one way seeds travel; other plants use animals, water or different shapes to spread their seeds.

Things to try

  • Keep the weight and breeze the same. Does a larger wing change where the seed lands?
  • Keep the wing the same. Compare a light seed with a heavy one.
  • Set the breeze to zero, then increase it. What changes about the flight?
  • Use autoplay to compare pairs, then slow the action to 0.1× to watch the spin.
  • Make a paper helicopter with folded blades and a paperclip. Drop it safely from the same height each time and compare your designs.
